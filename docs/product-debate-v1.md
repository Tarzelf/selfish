# Selfish — Product Debate & v1 Recommendation

Working name: **Selfish**
Form: tasteful 18+ iOS listening app
Thesis to pressure-test: high-fidelity ASMR-style AI+TTS. Two promises — focus, and exploring inner desire through voice. Primary audience: women. Not “Grok sexting with a female skin.” A private listening room: the audio equivalent of a well-written erotic novel plus a skilled intimate partner who listens.

This document argues both sides, then commits to one coherent v1. It is a product argument, not a spec for engineers to cargo-cult.

---

## What is actually being built (and where it is fragile)

The founder already knows spicy chat works — for men. That is a trap with a revenue graph attached. Male spicy chat is *performance plus novelty*: type, get filth, escalate, screenshot, churn, return. The loop is extractive and the user is the director.

The product described here is the opposite loop. She does not want to direct. She wants to be received. She wants language with rhythm, not a chatbot that asks what she’s wearing. She wants the lights off.

That is a harder product. Three places the idea is genuinely weak:

1. **Voice quality is the whole company.** If the TTS still sounds like a navigation system reading a love letter, no amount of typography will save it. Intimacy is a timbre problem before it is a language-model problem. Most “AI girlfriend” audio fails here and then papers over it with visuals and chat. This product has nowhere to hide.

2. **“Focus + Desire” is a beautiful insight and a muddy store listing.** The adjacency is real — the same nervous system that cannot concentrate is the one that cannot surrender. But Apple, partners glancing at a home screen, and a 32-year-old who is already wary will all ask: *is this Headspace or is this porn?* If the answer is “both,” many people hear “neither, and slightly embarrassing.”

3. **The market is not empty.** Dipsea, Quinn, Audible erotica, well-read fanfic, and a graveyard of “audio romance” apps already exist. “We have AI” is not a reason to switch. The only defensible difference is *how it feels to be in the room*: lean-back, aftercare, no performance, no visual meat. If v1 ships a character grid and a text box, it is the thing it swore not to be.

The idea is not weak as a feeling. It is weak as a default AI roadmap. Taste is a series of refusals.

## 1. Catalog vs live generative vs hybrid

### Catalog of professionally written scenes

**For.** This is how good erotic audio actually works. A writer who understands pacing, a voice that has somewhere to go, sound design that knows when to shut up. Runtime is honest. Aftercare can be composed, not improvised by a model that wants to keep talking. App Review can listen to the thing. Cost is predictable. Replay, for a well-made scene, is a feature — people reread novels.

**Against.** A library is a library. After six nights she has heard the house style. It is hard to feel *chosen*. The product starts to look like a smaller, pricier Quinn. The AI story you told investors becomes a footnote: “we used a model to help the writers.” Men who validated spicy chat will call this boring. They are not the user, but they will be in the room when you lose your nerve.

### Fully live generative conversation

**For.** Infinite nights. It can use her name, her pace, the thing she whispered once. It feels like a someone. Replay is structurally impossible in the best way. This is the only version that is obviously a new category rather than a nicer audiobook.

**Against.** This is how you accidentally build Grok sexting. Latency kills ASMR — a 900ms pause before “yes” is the sound of a server, not a mouth. Models hedge, then lurch, then get clinical, then get porn-scripted. They ask questions because questions are what chatbots know how to do, and every question is a demand that she perform. Safety is a nightmare in open generation (minors, real-world harm, celebrity, non-consent that isn’t clearly framed as fiction). App Review cannot “listen to the model.” Cost follows the people who stay in the dark for forty minutes. And the cruelest issue: generative intimacy currently sounds clever more often than it sounds *close*.

### Hybrid — guided ritual that becomes generative

**For.** You sell a container, not a prompt box. Arrival, descent, peak or stillness, return. The bones are written by humans who can write. The flesh can answer a whisper, stretch a silence, say her name, follow a single preference she set when she was dressed and not ashamed. She never has to be the dungeon master. You can guarantee aftercare because aftercare is a *stage*, not a vibe the model might remember. This is how a skilled partner actually works: they have a sense of the night, and they listen inside it.

**Against.** Hardest to build. Easy to fake — a meditation script with a fade to moan. Easy to ruin — a ritual that interrupts itself with “what do you want me to do next?” If the generative layer is timid, she will feel the seams. If it is bold, you inherit all the safety problems of live chat, just with nicer lighting.

### Verdict

Hybrid. Catalog-only is a respectable business and a coward’s version of this brief. Fully live is the founder’s ex. The product is a ritual with a pulse.

v1 should feel 80% composed, 20% alive. If she never speaks, the night is still complete. If she does, the night notices.

## 2. Focus and Desire: one app vs two

### One app

**For.** The insight is the pairing. Focus is not a second SKU; it is how she is allowed to arrive. A voice that has already sat with her attention has earned the right to sit with her desire. One habit. One subscription. One private room. Women already live this way — a romance novel on the train, a meditation app at 11, the same body both times. Splitting them is a male product manager’s idea of propriety.

**Against.** Apple does not grade insights. Mixing wellness-adjacent language with erotica is how you get a rejection, a 17+ listing that still feels dirty, and a brand that cannot be screenshotted. A partner sees “Selfish” and opens it to a dim red room. A user who came for focus feels bait-and-switched the first time Desire is visible. You will be tempted to use Focus as a trojan horse. That is sleazy, and she will feel it.

### Two apps

**For.** Clean store stories. Focus can be quiet and shippable. Desire can be honest about what it is. If Desire is banned, Focus survives. Families, shared iPads, and “what’s that app?” get easier. Brand risk is contained.

**Against.** Two apps is how both die. You split the writing, the voice, the subscription, the habit. You teach her that attention and desire are different products, which is the opposite of the thesis. The woman you want will not download a second app at 11:40 p.m. And “we made two apps so Apple would like us” is not a brand. It is fear with a bundle ID.

### Verdict

**One app, two weathers, one lock.** Not two companies. Not a wellness claim.

Do not call Focus “mental health,” “therapy,” or “meditation for healing.” Call it listening. No sleep-science theater, no HRV, no medical adjacent. Desire is not hidden behind a fake settings toggle, but it is not the icon, the first screenshot, or the first minute for a new user. The first night can be Focus or a closed-door Desire path she chooses in language that does not wink.

Brand risk is real. Handle it with metadata, a calm icon, and a home screen that does not look like a club. Do not handle it by lying, and do not handle it by forking the product.

## 3. Interactive conversation vs lean-back listening

### Interactive

**For.** Agency. Repair. “Harder.” “Slower.” “Don’t stop.” A partner who cannot hear is a recording. Some women *do* want to talk; some nights the talking *is* the desire. Interaction is how the product learns without a quiz. It is also the only story that makes “AI” true in the way press will want it to be true.

**Against.** This is where the male pattern sneaks in. Typing in bed. Being interesting. Feeding the machine so it will love you. Performance anxiety with better typography. Trance is a one-way street for long stretches; every UI affordance is a flashlight in the face. Voice input in a shared apartment is its own horror. And if interaction is the default, the quiet woman — your actual customer — will feel the product waiting on her, which is the opposite of being held.

### Lean-back

**For.** She came to receive. Hands off the phone. AirPods in. This is the novel, the radio play, the person who does not need her to host. It is more female in the way this brief means female: not a gender essentialism, a use-of-the-body. It is also more differentiated from every chatbot on earth.

**Against.** Lean-back without life is Audible with shame. Metrics people will hate it (session depth without messages looks like a podcast). Replay needs a catalog or a ritual engine. “She never spoke” is beautiful and, if you are not careful, indistinguishable from not caring.

### Verdict

**Lean-back is the product. Interaction is a courtesy, never a job.**

No text box on the primary surface. No “your turn.” If she wants to steer, she can tap a single, dim hold — *stay / slower / enough* — or speak one short phrase. Silence is a complete answer. The voice should be good at continuing without her. That is the skill.

## 4. Naming: “Selfish” — brilliant or toxic?

### Brilliant

Women are trained to be available. The name is the permission slip. It covers both promises without splitting into “Zen” and “Sin”: selfish with your attention, selfish with your pleasure. It is not floral, not a candle SKU, not *Lush / Ember / Bloom / Vesper*. It has a spine. The right woman will exhale when she sees it. That exhale is the brand.

### Toxic

It sounds like a finance bro podcast, a dating-app villain, or a mean-girl tote bag. On a home screen it is an argument waiting to happen. Some of the exact wary women you want will bounce — not because they don’t want the thing, but because they don’t want to *call themselves that*. Search is awkward. Press will pun. Internationally it will not travel. And if the visual system is even slightly cold or dominatrix-coded, the name becomes a sneer.

### Verdict

**Keep it.** Not as a joke and not as aggression — as a quiet dare.

The name is only toxic if the product performs selfishness as extraction (tokens, streaks, “come back and be worse”). If the product performs selfishness as *finally taking a night for her nervous system*, the word recontextualizes. Do not subtitle it “your AI girlfriend.” Do not make the icon a pair of lips.

If launch research shows a hard flinch from the primary audience, keep **Selfish** as the studio and give the app a quieter public name. Do not do that preemptively. Preemptive softening is how you get *Luma*.

## 5. Visual design: dark luxury vs soft warm sanctuary vs stark minimal

### Dark luxury

**For.** Night. Closed door. Adult without asking. Expensive. Works in OLED bedrooms. Desire does not have to apologize for being nocturnal.

**Against.** This is the default skin of every AI companion app: burgundy, gold, a woman in profile, hotel-bar loneliness. It reads as *for men*, even when the copy says otherwise. It also makes Focus look like a lie.

### Soft warm sanctuary

**For.** Skin, linen, lamp. The body already understands this room. Aftercare looks like it belongs. Women will forgive a lot of product sins for a feeling of not being hunted.

**Against.** Goop. Yoga studio. Baby. “Self-care” as a scented candle. If you add blush pink and a serif that has seen too many wedding invitations, the erotic writing will feel like it wandered into the wrong boutique.

### Stark minimal

**For.** Aesop, a good novel, nothing to hide. The UI gets out of the way, which is the only visual idea that serves lean-back listening. It photographs as taste.

**Against.** Cold. Punitive. A clinic. Minimalism without warmth is how you make her feel examined.

### Verdict

**A dim warm room, almost no chrome.**

Not a dungeon, not a spa, not a concept store. One lamp. Paper, not marble. Type that looks like a well-made book, not a lingerie ad. During playback the phone should nearly disappear — a slow breath of color, a way to stop, nothing that looks tappable for the sake of looking rich. Focus and Desire share the same room; Desire does not “go red.” If you change the temperature at all, change it the way a room changes when someone sits closer, not the way a nightclub app changes when you unlock Premium.

## 6. Onboarding: learning her desire without a sleazy quiz

A 40-question kink inventory is not thoroughness. It is a one-way mirror. It forces confession before trust, in a fluorescent hallway, while she is still deciding whether you are safe. It also produces a profile that is too specific too early — the model then performs her answers back like a waiter repeating an order.

What a good lover does: they do not hand you a clipboard. They set a tone. They offer a first night that can be left. They notice.

### Do this instead

**The first minute is a voice, not a form.** A short arrival. No account wall before a sample of the actual craft (legal/age-gate first, then sound). If the voice is wrong, the app is wrong; let her know immediately.

**Three doors, not a taxonomy.**
- How do you want to be met? *Held / undone / left in quiet.*
- Who is in the room? Two or three presences, heard as audio chips, not a dating grid of faces.
- How much story? *Almost none / a little / take me somewhere.*

That is enough to start a ritual. Everything else is learned.

**Learn from the night, not the survey.** What she finished. Where she stopped. Whether she stayed for aftercare. Whether she ever pressed *enough*. Optional, later, when she is not performing for a new relationship: a private note she can leave in her own words. One sentence. Not a fetish tree.

**Never ask her to name herself as a type.** No “sub,” no body-of-partner picker, no “describe your fantasy in this box so we can generate it.” If she wants to write, she can write *after* she has been treated well.

The devil’s advocate: this will feel underspecified to people who think personalization is a form. Good. You are not building a recommendation engine for porn tags. You are building a memory of how she likes to be spoken to.

## 7. Aftercare / come-down as a first-class feature

This is where taste lives. It is also where almost every erotic product reveals it does not like women.

Porn ends at climax because climax is the product. A skilled partner stays because the person is the product. If Selfish cannot stay, it is a content feed with a nicer conscience.

### What aftercare is here

Not a survey. Not “rate this scene.” Not an upsell. Not a journal prompt that turns her body into data while she is still shaking.

It is a composed fourth movement: the voice remains, the language slows, the ask disappears. She is not abandoned at the brightest moment. She can have silence with someone still there. She can be walked back to the room — water, the weight of the blanket, the fact of the door. Focus, in this architecture, is not a second app. It is what aftercare already knows how to do.

### Product rules

- Every Desire ritual has a come-down that cannot be skipped by the system. She can leave. The app cannot yank her to a paywall or a “you might also like.”
- Aftercare has no commerce in it. None.
- The next morning, if anything remains, it is quiet: *you were held*, not *you unlocked*.
- A “still here” affordance — five more minutes of presence with no plot — is more important than a new scene.
- If she pressed *enough* mid-scene, aftercare starts immediately, without sulking, without asking why.

If you only have the budget to make one thing unusually good, make this. Writers will want to spend it on the peak. Peaks are common. Returns are rare.

## 8. Subscription vs session packs

### Subscription

**For.** A room you have a key to. Apple-shaped. Habit-shaped. Lets you stop metering intimacy, which is morally and aesthetically important. Writers and voices can be paid like a practice, not like a vending machine.

**Against.** Paying monthly for desire produces guilt, especially if she used it twice. Unused subscriptions become a little accusation on the credit card. “AI girlfriend subscription” is a cultural joke she does not want to be in. Churn after the first month will be ugly if the catalog is thin.

### Session packs

**For.** Matches how some women actually use erotic audio: not daily, a night she chooses. Less identity (“I subscribe to this”) and more act (“I bought three nights”). Easier for the wary. Honest about occasion.

**Against.** Token energy. Cam-site economics. The moment you sell “nights” you are one UI mistake from selling minutes, and minutes are how you tell her she is on the clock. Packs also fight the Focus habit — attention products want to be around.

### Verdict

**Subscription, with a complete first night that is not a tease.**

Let her hear a full ritual — arrival through aftercare — before she pays. A clipped sample is the porn-site move. Offer an annual that feels like joining, not a discount gimmick.

Session packs can exist later as a gift or a cautious alternative. They are not v1, because v1 is trying to teach a room, not a purchase. Never sell minutes. Never sell “more intense” as a SKU. Intensity is a door she already chose, not a SKU she upgrades.

# v1 — One coherent product

## North star

A private listening room where a woman can be received — first in her attention, then, when she asks, in her desire — without performing, without being shown, without being left at the brightest moment.

If we need it in one line:

> She puts the phone face down. Someone stays.

## Who it is for

- Women, roughly late twenties to early forties, who are curious about audio intimacy and suspicious of the category.
- People who already understand novels, headphones, and closing a door.
- Someone who has tried visual porn and felt nothing, or worse; who has tried a chatbot and felt like a prompt engineer; who has tried meditation apps and felt managed.
- Listeners who want to *receive* language — literary, embodied, paced — not collect characters.

## Who it is not for

- Men seeking a girlfriend simulator, a waifu, or a second spicy chat. We will not twist the product to catch them. They have many homes.
- People looking for a kink marketplace, extreme edge play as a catalog, or user-generated rooms.
- Couples who want to play together (a different product, a different consent problem).
- Anyone who wants a face, a body, a video, or a feed.
- Users under 18. No exceptions, no “mature 17-year-old” energy, no youthful personas.

## Core loop

**Open → pass the door (age, already known) → the room offers tonight, not a library → she chooses a weather (Quiet / Held / Undone) and a presence → she lies down → a ritual of 12–25 minutes: arrival, descent, peak or stillness, return → aftercare that does not ask anything of her → she closes the app feeling accompanied, not extracted from.**

Feeling X, named plainly: **accompanied.** Not “satisfied” (too porn), not “empowered” (too campaign), not “zen” (too app). Accompanied. The night happened, and she was not alone in it, and she does not owe anyone a review.

Secondary loop, daytime or raw nights: the same room, Quiet weather, no erotic turn. Same voice. Same come-down. This is Focus without a second product.

## Information architecture (five screens)

1. **The door (home).** Almost empty. *Tonight.* A continuation if a ritual was left unfinished. A small, late link to the library of weathers — not a grid of covers. Account lives here, in the corner, ashamed of itself.

2. **The room (player).** The product. Voice, time as a feeling rather than a scrubber she must use, *stay / slower / enough*, and a way to leave that starts aftercare rather than a hard cut. No waveform porn. No lyrics karaoke.

3. **Presences.** Two or three voices. Heard, not seen. A short written line each, like a dedication, not a bio with hobbies. No ages. No “redhead, 24.”

4. **Notes.** Optional, private, hers. One thought after a night if she wants. Not social. Not a prompt to generate the next scene from her diary unless she explicitly asks later (not v1).

5. **House rules (settings).** Age and safety, subscription, download/offline, who can hear notifications (ideally: none), delete my listening, a clear explanation that Desire is fiction.

No discover tab. No following. No streaks. No shop.

## Voice / persona system

Not characters. **Presences.**

v1 ships **three**, maybe two if the third is not ready. Each is a way of being in the room, not a fictional girlfriend with a backstory about coffee and trauma.

- **Low and near.** Warm, unhurried, more breath than performance. The default for Held and Quiet.
- **Clear and narrative.** Someone who can take her somewhere — a hotel corridor, a kitchen at 2 a.m. — without sounding like an audiobook actor slumming. Default for story-forward nights.
- **Even and spare.** Less sweet, less gendered, comfortable with silence. For women who do not want to be mothered or “babygirl’d.”

Rules:

- No visual avatars. A color temperature and a name that could be a first name or a word. Not a model photo.
- She picks a presence the way she picks who is allowed to speak, not who she is dating.
- The generative layer may remember her name, her preferred weather, and what she asked to avoid. It does not invent a relationship status or get jealous. Jealousy is a chatbot trick.
- Writers own the ritual spine. The model fills, it does not author the night from zero.
- TTS is cast like a voice actor, not picked from a vendor dropdown of “Sexy Female 3.” If we cannot get three voices that survive a dark room and a good speaker, we ship one.

## Safety rails

- **18+ only.** Hard gate before any Desire audio. Account age, store rating 17+, no youthful language, no school/teen/“barely” framing, no voices that read as girls. This is non-negotiable and not a tone.
- **Consent in the product, not just the ToS.** She can stop. *Enough* is sacred. Aftercare after stop. No punishment, no “are you sure you don’t want…”
- **Fiction, labeled.** Desire is fantasy. Copy says so once, calmly, in House rules and the first Desire night — not as a wink and not as a legal dump in the first breath of audio.
- **Fantasy negotiation is allowed; real-world harm is not.** Fictional power, fictional force, fictional transgression — if she chose Undone and the scene is clearly a story. No instructions for harming actual people. No non-consent content that is framed as a how-to, a memory of a real assault, or a thing to do tonight to someone who has not agreed. The model is not a planner.
- **No minors in any layer.** Prompts, generation, user notes, titles. Refuse and end the ritual. Do not lecture.
- **No real people.** No celebrity voices, no cloning a partner, no “make it sound like my ex.”
- **Privacy as manners.** No push that could light up a lock screen with the plot. No social graph. Deleting history is easy and complete.
- **Human write-down of disallowed territories** for v1 (not an exhaustive moral encyclopedia — a production list): anything involving anyone 17 or under; bestiality; real-world incest with living family framed as real; sexual violence as instruction. Literary darkness in clearly fictional Undone rituals can exist later; v1 stays this side of the line so the room feels safe enough to enter.

## What we will not build in v1

- Text chat as a primary surface.
- Video, generated images, bodies, faces, “see her.”
- A character marketplace or creator store.
- User-generated scenes or a community.
- Voice cloning of real individuals.
- Couples mode, share links, or “listen together.”
- Health claims, sleep scores, streaks, mindfulness theater.
- A 40-question kink quiz or a tag-based porn taxonomy.
- Open “describe anything” generation.
- Male-seeking girlfriend features (memory of a romance arc, jealousy, “good morning text”).
- Notifications that create hunger. If we notify at all, it is a thing she scheduled.
- Minutes, tokens, or pay-per-moan.
- Web. This is a phone in a dark room.

The thing we will be most tempted to add, and must not: a clever chat so the demo looks like AI.

## User review persona

**Use this person as a critic of the actual product.** If she would not write the review below after a first listen, the build is wrong — even if the metrics are fine.

### Mara, 32

Mara lives in a one-bedroom she is proud of and slightly behind on. She works in a job that requires her to be pleasant on purpose. She reads at night, or used to, before the scroll won. She has an Audible account she resents and a meditation app she opened for eleven days in January. She has watched porn that made her feel like she was interrupting someone. A friend sent her a chatbot screenshot once and she felt secondhand embarrassment so strong she laughed it off and thought about it later, alone, with a kind of anger — not at sex, at the *neediness* of the interface.

She is not vanilla as a person and not adventurous as a customer. She will not complete a kink quiz. She will not put a face on her home screen that she has to explain. She will pay for something that feels like a well-made object. She will delete something that feels like it is trying to get her pregnant with a subscription.

She has AirPods. She locks the bedroom door even when no one else is home. She wants to want, and she wants to not be seen wanting, and she is almost ready to admit those are the same wish.

### First-listen review (200 words)

I almost didn’t download it because of the name. Selfish sounded like a dare from someone I wouldn’t like. The icon was quiet enough that I stopped arguing with myself.

I close those apps the second they ask what I’m into. This one didn’t. It asked how I wanted to be met, and let me hear the voice first — a person in the next room, not a catalog of girls. I picked “held” because “undone” felt like lying.

I put the phone face down. I was sure it would get breathy and stupid. It didn’t, not in the way I meant. Slower than I expected. A story only in the sense that someone knew where we were. I didn’t have to talk. I kept waiting for my turn — the little box, the “your move” — and it never came. That was the thing I didn’t know I needed: not being a good user.

The end surprised me. It didn’t drop me. It stayed until my hands felt like my hands again. I don’t know if I’ll use the other side of it. I might. I didn’t feel managed, or like I’d wandered into a man’s idea of a woman’s secret. I felt allowed to take a night. That’s all I wanted, and I never find it.

## Closing

Build the room. Hire the writers. Cast the voices. Compose the return. Let the model live inside a ritual, not the other way around.

If the first demo needs a text field to be impressive, the first demo is of the wrong product.
