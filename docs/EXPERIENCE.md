# The experience: discreet outside, addictive inside

> Product answer to: *what emotion does the first listen produce, and why would she open this twice in one day?*

## The mistake we made

We built a **wellness catalog of literary slow-burn**. That produces the second emotion — held, supported, happy — which is real and belongs in the app as **aftercare and Rest**. It is not the reason a woman opens this on a Tuesday afternoon and again at 11pm.

Spicy that isn't spicy is just romance with better typography. Quinn's users don't stay for "may I kiss you goodnight." They stay because a specific voice, in their ear, saying a specific thing, works on their body — and they can get that feeling again tonight without hunting.

**Success, defined:**

| Mode | First-listen emotion | Daily behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Close** (spicy) | Arousal. Breath changes. She stays through the loop. | Opens 1–3×/day the way people use erotica: short, private, same voice, slight variation |
| **Warm** (slow-burn) | Tension, being wanted, the movie in her head | Evening ritual, series, longer sessions |
| **Soft / Rest** | Safe, held, sleepy | Nightly wind-down — the cover story *and* a real product |

If Close doesn't produce the first column, the app is a pretty sleep app with a secret it never keeps.

## The two-room architecture

**Room 1 — what the world sees.** App name, icon, lock screen, App Store screenshots, the landing page if a coworker glances. Sleep-and-stories. "Time that's just for you." Nothing dripping. This is not camouflage-as-cowardice; it is the product's brake-removal (Nagoski) and its App Store survival.

**Room 2 — what she walks into after she opts Close.** Almost empty. One voice. One play. The loop she used last time, already cued. Under it: *Again · Slower · Closer · After.* No catalog wall. No "how do you want to feel?" quiz. She did not open this to browse.

Outsiders know nothing. Insiders crave the walk back into room 2.

## The unit of value is a loop, not a story

Stories (8–20 min, plot, dinner parties) are **Warm**. They are for when she has time and wants narrative.

**Close** is a different format:

- 3–7 minutes
- No plot. Second person. He is already in the room.
- ASMR dirty talk: breath, proximity, praise, want, what he's doing, what he wants her to do
- Written to be **replayed**, not finished-and-shelved
- Ships as a **family of 4 recycles**, not one track:
  - **Again** — the take that worked
  - **Slower** — same words, half the pace, more breath
  - **Closer** — same dynamic, more heat, still her hard limits
  - **After** — 90 seconds of aftercare from the same mouth (the supported feeling, earned)

She does not need 400 stories. She needs **12 loops × 4 recycles × 6 voices** she actually returns to. Depth in three dynamics: praise, boyfriend-in-your-ear, soft-authority. That's 144 close takes — and she will live in about 8 of them.

This is what "varieties they can literally recycle" means. Not a content firehose. A private radio station with four buttons.

## The ritual (home, after Close is on)

```
          SELFISH

     He's already here.

           [ ▶ ]
      Jasper · Stay right there
           4 min

     Again    Slower    Closer    After

     — other mouths —
     [J] [E] [R] [N] …
```

One thumb. Headphones on. No decisions.

- First session of the day: the loop she finished last, or the house pick for her favorite voice
- Mid-loop: she can switch recycle without leaving
- After it ends: it does **not** disappear. It offers Again. That's the multiple-times-a-day hook.
- Stories and Rest live one tab over, for when she wants them. They are not the front door.

## What we will not do

- Lead with "AI girlfriend chat." That's Apple's 4.3 graveyard and a different product.
- Put explicit titles on the home screen a roommate can read from the couch. Cards stay suggestive; the heat is in the audio.
- Surprise her. Hard limits still win. Close is opt-in. Aftercare is always one tap away — that's how you get both dripping *and* supported, in that order.
- Generate live from her typing. Recycles are pre-rendered. The feeling of "made for tonight" comes from the four buttons, not a prompt box.

## How we know it's working

Not downloads. Not time-in-app vanity.

1. **Close loop completion rate** > 70% on first play (if they bounce at 0:40, the take is not spicy enough or the voice is wrong).
2. **Same-loop replay within 24h** — the recycle metric. If this is low, we have a catalog, not a habit.
3. **Same-voice share of weekly listens** — parasocial lock. If they bounce across voices, we don't have a Jasper yet.
4. **Disclosure-aware retention** — they know it's synthetic and still come back. The Dipsea lesson.

Until (2) moves, we are still generic.

## Implemented in this preview

- Close catalog: 6 loops × 4 recycles (Stay, Right Here, The List, Quiet, Come Here, Still). Titles roommate-safe; heat in the audio.
- Tonight becomes the ritual when heat is Close: one play, last loop cued, Again / Slower / Closer / After, other mouths. Stories move to Browse.
- Soft / Warm keep the editorial Tonight. Close loops are invisible until she opts in.
- Finished loops stay on the rail. Aftercare is one tap, same mouth.
- Onboarding heat is honest: Soft / Warm / Close, not Comfort / Slow burn / Spicy.
- Real Close-register take: `Stay` (Grok/castor) plus `After` from the same mouth. Grok accepted the dirty-talk script.
