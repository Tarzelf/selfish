## 1. EU AI Act Article 50 (applicable since Aug 2, 2026)

We are both **provider** (self-hosted Orpheus/Chatterbox TTS = an AI system generating synthetic audio) and **deployer** (publishing voice-clone deepfakes). The Act applies extraterritorially to a US company whose AI outputs are used in the EU (Art. 2(1)(c)); web availability in the EU triggers it. Penalties: up to **€15M or 3% of worldwide turnover** ([Cooley](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-1904691/)).

- **Art. 50(2) provider duty:** outputs must be (a) *marked in machine-readable format* and (b) *detectable*. The Commission's **Guidelines C(2026) 5054 (adopted 20 July 2026)** accept watermarks, metadata identification, cryptographic provenance (C2PA-style), fingerprints, or combinations; a multi-layered approach (signed metadata + imperceptible audio watermark, e.g., AudioSeal-class) is recommended ([Guidelines PDF](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/guidelines_on_the_implementation_of_the_transparency_obligations_for_certain_ai_systems_under_article_50_of_the_ai_act_bzptwqhk0ikg1dtlddap41psfy_131215.pdf), paras 71–87). A **detection means** must be available to listeners. The voluntary **[Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content)** is the recognized compliance framework. Systems placed on the EEA market **on/after Aug 2, 2026 must comply immediately** ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august)). ElevenLabs output remains *our* responsibility even if they mark it upstream.
- **Art. 50(4) deployer duty / artistic carve-out:** narrations in a real, licensed narrator's recognizable voice are **deepfakes** (Art. 3(60)). Fictional audio stories qualify as "evidently… fictional works," so the carve-out **applies**: disclosure reduces to an unobtrusive notice (episode description, credits, loading screen) — no in-audio interruption ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/taking-the-eu-ai-act-to-practice-reading-the-commissions-draft-article-50-guidelines)). The carve-out softens *disclosure form only*. **Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking has no artistic carve-out — watermark regardless.**

## 2. UK Online Safety Act

**Audio-only erotica is covered.** Ofcom's Part 5 guidance: regulated pornographic content "encompasses… still and moving images, **audio** and audio-visual content," explicitly including content **generated by an automated tool in response to a user prompt** ([Ofcom Part 5 guidance](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-1-10-weeks/272586-consultation-guidance-for-service-providers-publishing-pornographic-content/associated-documents/annex-2-guidance-for-service-providers-publishing-pornographic-content-online), §3.7). As publisher of our own content we are a **Part 5 service**; duty in force since **17 Jan 2025**: **highly effective age assurance (HEAA)** before any pornographic content is encountered. Accepted: photo-ID matching, facial age estimation, mobile/credit-card checks, digital ID, open banking; **self-declaration is non-compliant**. Costs ~**£0.10–£0.25 per facial-age-estimation check** ([Yoti pricing](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-13/documents/702818/615862164889583-pricing-document-2022-05-18-1346.pdf)). **Enforcement reaches small foreign services:** Ofcom fined AVS Group £1M, 8579 LLC £1.35M, Kick Online £800k; Itai Tech resolved by **geoblocking the UK** ([Ofcom enforcement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/enforcement-programme-to-protect-children-from-encountering-pornographic-content-through-the-use-of-age-assurance)). Decision required: HEAA for UK users, or geoblock the UK at launch.

## 3. US state age-verification laws

Do **not** assume audio is out of scope. After [FSC v. Paxton](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/606/23-1122/) (June 2025, upholding Texas HB 1181), ~24 states have similar laws:

- **Texas HB 1181**: covers "**descriptions**… of sexual intercourse," not just depictions — explicit audio plausibly counts ([bill text](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88r/billtext/pdf/hb01181f.pdf)).
- **Tennessee (Protect TN Minors Act)**: expressly covers "**Text, audio**, imagery, or video"; **Florida §501.1737**: "depicts or **describes**"; Minnesota: "text, **sound recording**…".
- Most trigger at **>⅓ (or 25%) of site content** being harmful-to-minors material — a dedicated erotica service exceeds any threshold.

**Conclusion:** plan age verification (government ID or transactional-data method, zero retention) in Texas, Tennessee, Florida, Utah, Louisiana and similar states from day one; the same vendor integration serves the UK.

## 4. Voice licensing

- **Tennessee ELVIS Act** (July 2024): protects **voice**; liability also for making available algorithms whose primary purpose is producing an individual's voice without authorization.
- **California AB 2602** (since Jan 2025): a digital-replica clause is **unenforceable** without a "**reasonably specific description** of intended uses" and performer counsel/union representation ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/californias-new-ai-laws-limit-uses-of-digital-likeness)). Boilerplate "all media now known or hereafter devised" **fails**. **AB 1836** (deceased personalities) requires estate consent.
- **NO FAKES Act (S.4591)**: out of Senate Judiciary June 2026, **not yet law**.
- **Lehrman v. Lovo** (S.D.N.Y.): right-of-publicity and contract claims allowed to proceed (July 2025); stayed May 2026 on Lovo's bankruptcy ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/voice-actors-can-pursue-some-claims-over-ai-voiceovers-us-court-says-2025-07-10/)). State publicity claims are viable; consent scope is everything.
- **License must contain:** explicit, reasonably specific description of use in **AI-generated erotic audio** (named app, content ratings); consent to fine-tuning on the actor's recordings; per-use or revenue-based compensation; term/territory; approval/exclusion rights over content categories; counsel/union acknowledgment (AB 2602); scoped moral-rights waiver; post-termination takedown terms.

## 5. FTC

**Operation AI Comply** (2024): "no AI exemption" from Section 5. Sept 2025: **6(b) study of AI companion chatbots** (Character.AI, Meta, OpenAI, Snap, xAI…) focused on disclosures, sexual content, minors, monetization ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)) — intimacy apps are squarely on the radar; California SB 243 (Oct 2025) already mandates AI-companion disclosures. Practical rule: no need to shout "AI" in every ad, but never state or imply narrators personally recorded a story, no fabricated human personas in reviews (Fake Reviews Rule), narrator bios must not read as human performance. Safe pattern: **"Narrated in the voice of [Actor], AI-rendered under license."** Never deny AI use if asked in-app.
