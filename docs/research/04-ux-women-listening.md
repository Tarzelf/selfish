## Onboarding (not a kink quiz)

**Goal:** Enough signal to personalize; zero medical/clinical vibe.

1. Age gate (18+) — clear, serious, no cute jokes.
2. "What do you want this to feel like?" — 4–6 mood cards (e.g. *Held*, *Teased*, *Powerful*, *Soft*, *Slow burn*, *Spoken to*).
3. Voice preference — 2–3 cast options (warm low, soft breathy, steady calm) with 5s samples.
4. Intensity default — 1 Quiet … 5 Explicit (labels: *Suggestive / Warm / Spicy / Explicit / Raw*).
5. Privacy promise — one screen: encrypted preferences, no ads on your desire, discrete billing.

Skip-any-step allowed after age gate. Learn more from in-session feedback ("more of this" / "less of this").

## Voice UX — erotic vs creepy

### Erotic (aim)
- Warm proximity; natural breath; unhurried cadence.
- Specific sensory detail without clinical anatomy dumps.
- Consent and check-ins woven into fiction ("tell me if you want me to slow down").
- Silence as tension; not constant talking.
- Second-person ("you") for immersion *when she opts into being addressed*; third-person narrative as safer default for some moods.

### Creepy (avoid)
- Sudden volume spikes, uncanny moan loops, forced dirty talk early.
- Impersonating a real ex/celebrity.
- Ignoring stop / talking over pause.
- Infantilizing or degrading language unless she explicitly chose that lane.
- Chatbot personality breaking the spell ("As an AI…").

### Script structure (pattern only — no sample erotica here)
1. Settle (environment, breath, permission).
2. Want (being seen / anticipated).
3. Build (pacing aligned to intensity).
4. Peak (optional; intensity-gated).
5. Aftercare (warmth, dignity, soft exit).

ASMR craft: soft consonants, close-mic feel, limited sound bed (rain/room tone optional), avoid cartoon SFX.

## Intensity + consent mechanics

- **Intensity dial** always reachable (lock-screen friendly controls later).
- **Stop** = immediate fade, no guilt copy ("Stopped. You're in control.").
- **Safeword-style soft stop:** "Pause scene" vs "End night."
- **Hard policy layer** (invisible): block minors, non-consent defaults, real-person cloning.
- **Feedback chips** after session: Too fast / Too soft / Perfect / Wrong mood — trains personalization without a form.

---

## Focus mode vs Desire mode

**Recommendation: One product, two temperatures — not two apps.**

- Marketing leads with **desire / intimacy listening** (honest wedge).
- In-product, "Settle" or "Quiet mind" is the brake-lowering prelude and a valid short session on its own (Bloom/Ferly insight).
- Do **not** market as productivity/focus app — that dilutes trust and App Store story without solving distribution for spicy generative content.

## Session 1 success criteria

She should be able to say:
1. "That felt like it was for me" (personalization).
2. "I didn't feel gross afterward" (taste + aftercare).
3. "I knew I could stop" (agency).
4. Opens again within 7 days without a push notification guilt trip.

Instrument: completion ≥60%, "for me" ≥4/5, D1 return ≥25% among finishers (directional targets for web MVP).

## Privacy UX

- Optional Face ID / PIN to open app.
- Neutral lock-screen player title ("Listening" / brand only).
- Session history local-first or encrypted; easy wipe.
- Export/delete account in settings.
- No screenshot-sensitive text left on screen after session (fade transcript if shown).

---

## Name critique: "Selfish"

**Pros:** Reclaims desire; memorable; fits "tonight is yours"; adult without being anatomical.  
**Cons:** Can read as cold, male-coded, or judgmental before the reframe lands; App Store/search optics mixed.

**Test line:** "Selfish — a private voice for your desire."  
If 10 target users flinch, shortlist: *Private*, *Undone*, *After Hours*, *Low Voice*, *Hush*, *Only You*.

## Design direction (for later UI)

- Night intimacy, not purple-AI SaaS; not terracotta wellness cliché.
- Full-bleed atmosphere (soft gradient / abstract light), brand as hero.
- Typography expressive but quiet; motion: slow fades, breathing pulse on play.
- Cards only for mood selection (interaction), not content shelves in the first viewport.
