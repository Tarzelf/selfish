# Selfish — Market Research Brief

**Working name:** Selfish  
**Scope:** Product/market research only (no implementation)  
**Date:** 2026-08-14  
**Thesis under test:** High-fidelity AI + TTS ASMR-style listening can beat static erotic audio libraries for women by personalizing desire exploration (and secondarily supporting focus).

---

## 1. Executive verdict

The women-led erotic audio category is real, priced, and proven at roughly mid–single-digit millions ARR for category leaders (Dipsea ~$6M ARR / ~94k paying subs as of late 2024; Quinn publicly cited as majority-women, ~$8/mo). The **core white space is not “another spicy library”** — Dipsea/Quinn/Bloom already own curation and creator range. The bet that matters is **personalized generative audio**: studio polish *without* a fixed catalog ceiling. That lane is **already contested by early AI-audio products (Hearr, Velvet, The Private Story) and by AI-text desire tools (Melt)**, while Bloom is bolting AI chat onto a library. Selfish wins only if voice quality + emotional pacing + private desire discovery feel *closer to a human whisper than to a chatbot reading smut* — and if brand/App Store framing stays fiction/wellness, not porn UI.

**Critical caution:** Founder proof that “spicy Grok chat for men is scarily good” is weak evidence for a women-first *listening* product. Male-oriented AI NSFW markets optimize for explicitness, speed, and novelty; women-first audio winners optimize for story, consent cues, emotional intimacy, agency, and voice trust. Transfer risk is high.

## 4. The gap: curated studio audio ↔ interactive generative voice

```
STATIC LIBRARY                         GENERATIVE DESIRE
(Dipsea polish)                        (Chatbots / Melt text)
     |                                          |
     |     ★ contested white space ★            |
     |   Personalized high-fidelity LISTENING   |
     |   (ASMR voice + story + agency)          |
     |                                          |
Creator range (Quinn) -------- Wellness brakes (Ferly)
```

### What curated studio wins today
- **Trust in the ear:** professional performers; intimacy coordinators becoming industry talk; “will this be good?” anxiety removed.
- **Editorial taste:** consent modeling, pacing, yearning, aftercare — the stuff press repeatedly cites as why women prefer audio to tube porn.
- **Session reliability:** open app → play → arousal path works at 11pm.
- **Brand safety wrapper:** romance / wellness packaging that keeps Apple/Meta distribution alive.

### What curated studio cannot do
- Match **personal fantasy specificity** (Lehmiller-style fantasy diversity >> any catalog).
- Remember *your* edges, pace, names, dynamics across nights.
- Turn “I don’t know what I want” into a private exploration loop without endless scrolling.

### What generative AI chat wins
- Infinite specificity; interactive agency; “scarily good” for users who like typing fantasy.
- Continuity / memory (when product invests in it).

### What generative chat loses for this thesis
- **Listening immersion** (ASMR, breath, timing, sound bed) is the product, not a side feature.
- Women-first winners sell *imagination + voice presence*, not porn-chat dopamine loops.
- Uncanny / repetitive LLM smut fatigues fast → **churn**.

### Where AI + TTS can win
1. **Specificity without hunt:** generate the scene Quinn would take 20 minutes to find (or never has).
2. **Agency with low effort:** dials for intensity, POV, consent boundaries, pace — closer to “choose your own night” than prompt engineering.
3. **Private desire literacy:** guided questions that feel like Ferly’s insight but resolve into a *hot listen*, not a worksheet.
4. **Dual-use ASMR/focus:** non-explicit modes that normalize daily open rates (habit) while spicy modes monetize deeper intent — *if* brand doesn’t split into two mediocre apps.
5. **Cost structure:** avoid Dipsea’s writers/actors/sound design COGS at the margin (though premium voice still costs; Dipsea already touching ElevenLabs).

### Where AI + TTS loses (today)
1. **Voice trust:** press quotes from performers (“AI could never” for intimate delivery) reflect a real ear test. Breath, laugh, micro-timing, vulnerability ≠ average TTS.
2. **Story craft:** yearning/slow-burn is culturally winning; LLM default is over-eager explicitness (male-porn pacing).
3. **Incumbents moving:** Dipsea has AI voice tooling access; Bloom has AI chat; Quinn has cultural lock on “internet boyfriend” voices.
4. **Distribution:** generative explicit audio is closer to Apple’s “pornography” nerve than pre-vetted fiction libraries. Bloom already routes hotter content to web.
5. **Early twins exist:** Hearr/Velvet prove the idea is obvious in 2026 — differentiation must be craft + brand + iOS product quality, not “we use AI.”

**Honest gap size:** Real, but **narrowing and quality-constrained**. The gap is not “AI audio doesn’t exist”; it is “nobody has yet shipped Dipsea-grade *taste* with Melt-grade *personalization* in a women-coded iOS listening ritual.”

## 5. Women-user JTBD (jobs to be done)

Derived from NYT / Independent / NBC / Stylist coverage, clinical framing (responsive desire, dual-control), and product patterns of winners.

### Primary jobs
1. **Help me get turned on without visual porn** — avoid body comparison, male-gaze staging, ethical ick.

5. **Match my exact mood tonight** — soft / spicy / queer / power / comfort — with low search friction.
6. **Feel safe: consent, enthusiasm, boundaries modeled** — clicking locks, verbal yes, care after intensity.

### Secondary / adjacent jobs
7. **Quiet my head enough to feel anything** (Ferly/Bloom guided) — brakes down before accelerator.
8. **Get in the mood before partnered sex** — solo listen as bridge, not only masturbation aid.
9. **Feel sexy again** (postpartum, stress, long-term relationship drift).

| Male-porn pattern (common in NSFW AI) | Women-audio pattern (winners) |
|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Explicit acts fast | Build, yearning, delayed gratification |

**Product implication:** If Selfish ports male Grok-spicy defaults into a “for women” skin, it will lose to Quinn/Dipsea on trust even if generative. The model must be steered toward **romance-literate erotica craft**, not ERP transcript dumps.

## 6. Opportunity thesis: where AI+TTS wins vs loses

### Win conditions (must be true)
- **Ear test passes:** users say voice feels intimate, not synthetic-narration.
- **Taste layer above the model:** editorial system prompts, intensity curves, consent scaffolding, anti-creep filters.
- **Personalization UX without prompt hell:** questionnaires, chips, “more like this,” memory of boundaries — Melt/Hearr pattern, refined.
- **Clear primary job:** either *desire exploration listen* or *focus ASMR* as hero; the other as mode — not equal dual thesis.
- **iOS distribution strategy:** fiction/wellness framing; age gates; possibly web for hottest tier (Bloom pattern).
- **Retention mechanic:** saved preferences + continuing story arcs + voice familiarity > one-off novelty gens.

### Lose conditions (likely failure modes)
- TTS uncanny valley during climax/intimacy moments.
- Feels like “AI girlfriend for girls” (stigma + App Store + brand collapse).
- Catalog competitors add “custom episode” features first with better voices.
- Acquisition CAC impossible: Meta/Apple ads hostile to adult creative; Dipsea-like organic storytelling hard without cultural founders.
- Churn: generative novelty week 1, sameness week 3 (classic AI companion failure).
- Name/brand “Selfish” reads as clever to founders, **cold or mean** to women seeking care/intimacy — needs testing.

### Strategic positioning options (pick one)
1. **“Dipsea, but it’s about you”** — generative studio taste; safest brand rhyme; compete on personalization.
2. **“Private desire coach that speaks”** — Ferly insight → Quinn heat; wellness + fantasy bridge.
3. **“ASMR focus that can go deeper”** — habit wedge, erotic unlock; risky dilution / policy.
4. **“Anti-chatbot listening ritual”** — explicitly *not* a companion; a curated generative session player.

Recommendation for thesis integrity: **(1) or (4)**. Dual-purpose focus+desire is a GTM story, not a v1 product architecture.

## 7. Brand / positioning language

### Language that works (tasteful, category-native)
- Spicy, steamy, romantic fiction, female gaze, intimate, immersive, soft-spoken, aftercare, praise, slow burn, safe space, explore desire, unwind, private
- Wellness-adjacent without medical claims: reconnect, feel wanted, imagination, listening
- Product metaphors that won press: “Headspace, but make it horny”; “HBO for your ears”; “Spotify for erotica”

### Language that slides sleazy
- Porn, nut, goon, hentai, “uncensored AI girlfriend,” fake nudes, “no limits,” extreme fetish billboards
- Visual body UI, neon pink/black porn chrome, dripping icons, endless kink grid as home screen
- Pushy upsells mid-arousal; streak guilt; parasocial “I miss you baby” companion retention hacks copied from male AI GF apps

### Must-not-do list for brand taste
1. **Don’t look like porn.** If the first screen could sit next to tube-site UI, you’ve lost the women-first category and likely Apple.
2. **Don’t lead with “uncensored.”** Lead with craft, privacy, and imagination; allow heat as a dial.
3. **Don’t anthropomorphize as a needy boyfriend bot** unless that’s the product (it’s a different, more stigmatized market).
4. **Don’t use women’s bodies as packaging.** Voice, typography, atmosphere > lingerie hero shots.
5. **Don’t joke-cruel brand.** “Selfish” needs soft framing (permission to prioritize your pleasure) or it reads as insult.
6. **Don’t ship male-gaze power fantasy as default.** Offer power exchange as opt-in with consent UX.
7. **Don’t dark-pattern shame for churn.** Erotic wellness brands die when they feel manipulative.
8. **Don’t overclaim clinical outcomes.** Wellness *wrapper* ≠ medical device.
9. **Don’t bury privacy.** Desire data is nuclear; say what is stored, what trains, what never leaves device/account.
10. **Don’t make intensity the only quality metric.** Yearning and care are features.

## 8. Risks

### Stigma
- Users may pay and love privately while refusing to talk/share → weak viral loops; reliance on TikTok/IG soft-wellness creative (fragile, policy-prone).
- Partner discovery / shared-phone anxiety → need discretionary app name, icon, lock.

### Churn
- Library apps churn when “I’ve heard the good ones.”
- Generative apps churn when “it all sounds the same” or voice breaks immersion.
- Mitigations: memory, series arcs, improving preference model, limited “signature voices,” human editorial drops mixed with gen.

### Content moderation
- Generative systems will attempt illegal/CSAM-adjacent, non-con, real-person deepfake, extreme harm content — **zero-tolerance filters required**.
- UGC prompts are a moderation surface even without creator uploads.
- Audit logs, refusal classes, and age assurance are table stakes (Apple’s creator/UGC rules trending stricter).

### Platform policy (Apple especially)
- Guideline **1.1.4** bans overtly sexual/pornographic material intended to stimulate erotic feelings — yet Dipsea/Quinn exist via **fiction/romance/wellness framing** and careful store listing language.
- UGC “used primarily for porn” can be removed without notice.
- Sharing personal data with third-party AI requires clear consent (2025+ guideline direction).
- Practical pattern: **softer App Store build + fuller web experience** (Bloom). Plan this on day one.
- Payment: IAP 15–30% tax; adult-adjacent refund/chargeback risk.

### Competitive / strategic
- Dipsea under RevenueCat may get sharper subscription ops and AI production scaling.
- Quinn owns culture and celebrity voice moments AI cannot buy easily.
- EU generative audio startups already pitching the exact Selfish sentence.

### Founder-fit risk
- Male-user Grok chat success ≠ women listening market.
- Maintaining “tasteful + easy to maintain” conflicts with needing heavy editorial/moderation ops for generative adult AI.

## 9. Open questions for the founder

1. **Primary job in one sentence:** Is Selfish a desire-exploration product, a focus/ASMR product, or truly both? Which one loses if you must cut?
2. **Who is she specifically?** Age, relationship status, Quinn vs Dipsea vs Ferly current behavior, spicy book reader or not?
3. **How personal is personal?** Questionnaire → episode (Hearr) vs continuous companion memory vs branching series?
4. **Voice strategy:** licensed human voice models, bespoke recorded latents, or commodity TTS? What is the ear-test bar to ship?
5. **Heat ceiling on iOS:** Will v1 be App Store–safe romance-spicy with web unlock — or full explicit on-device?
6. **Name test:** Does “Selfish” pass with target women in 5-second exposure, or does it need a softer public name?
7. **Competitive wedge vs Hearr/Velvet:** Why do you win in 12 months when they already say “generated for you, spoken to you”?
8. **Male expansion:** Feature or poison pill for brand trust with primary audience?
9. **Data ethics:** Do prompts ever train foundation models? Can she delete her desire profile forever?
10. **Unit economics:** COGS per 15-min gen audio at target quality; can annual sub à la Dipsea ($70) work or is metered required?
11. **Taste system:** Who is the editorial brain — founder intuition, romance writers, sex educators, eval set of “good nights”?
12. **Proof beyond male Grok chat:** What is the smallest women-listener experiment that falsifies the thesis?

## 11. Bottom line for product strategy

**Do not build another spicy library.** Build a **private generative listening ritual** with Dipsea-level taste controls and Quinn-level fantasy coverage — or don’t bother. The market will pay ~$8–13/mo; it will not forgive synthetic intimacy that feels cheap, male-paced, or policy-reckless. Treat Hearr/Velvet as the real competitive set, Dipsea/Quinn as the brand/taste bar, and male NSFW chat success as a cautionary analogy rather than a blueprint.
